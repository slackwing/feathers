So, do you need permits for every place? No. You need a reservation or permit only for: developed campgrounds (state parks, KOAs, etc.), national park camping or timed-entry where it applies (e.g., Rocky Mountain NP runs timed-entry permits on Recreation.gov in summer), and a handful of special-designation public lands. Rest areas, store lots, and standard dispersed BLM/forest land under 14 days need nothing.

What if you don't get there in time? This is the most important strategic point, so build your nights around it. The no-reservation, 24/7 options — rest areas, Walmart/truck stops, dispersed BLM — do not care when you arrive; there's no check-in window to miss. Reserved campgrounds are the opposite: some have gate-closing times and some will release your site if you no-show. So the move is to never make a reserved campground your only plan for a given night. Either call the campground about late arrival, or keep a 24/7 fallback (a known Walmart or rest area nearby) as Plan B for each leg. That single habit removes almost all the "we drove too far today" stress.

## Key constraint surfaced

Some sites (e.g., Black Canyon of the Gunnison's South Rim Drive) require RVs to be **less than 22 ft in length** — at 24 ft, we're over that limit. Worth surfacing per-stop on the site.

## Five categories of overnight parking

1. **Public land (BLM / National Forests)** — dispersed camping, up to 14 days, free, no reservation. Most abundant in AZ/NM/CO along I-10/I-40/I-25. Dries up east of the Plains.
2. **Highway rest areas** — free, no reservation, state-by-state time limits (CA: 8hr strict; AZ: no time limit but no camping outside RV; NM: 6–12hr; CO: variable).
3. **Private lots** — Walmart, Cracker Barrel, Cabela's, Bass Pro, Harvest Hosts, private truck stops. Manager's discretion; call ahead with license plate. Critical once past CO.
4. **Reserved campgrounds** — state parks, KOAs, etc. Require reservations, gate-closing times, no-show risk. Use for amenities/water hookups only.
5. **City streets** — strictest, last resort.

## Parking vs. camping — the cross-cutting rule

- **Parking** = staying *inside* the van (sleeping, doors closed). Broadly tolerated/legal.
- **Camping** = setting up *outside* the van (awning, chairs, grill, leveling jacks, slide-out). Frequently banned even where overnight parking is allowed.
- The vehicle boundary is the dividing line. A buttoned-up Sprinter is in the best legal position.
